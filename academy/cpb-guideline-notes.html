What does the guideline cover?

Multidisciplinary recommendations for safe adult cardiac-surgery CPB: the heart–lung circuit, monitoring, organ protection, anticoagulation, flow and perfusion, anaesthetic management, separation from bypass, and special procedures. It includes recommendation classes and levels of evidence. Read the full source and contents.

Module 1 · Monitoring (section 6)

In one sentence: CPB safety requires direct circuit and patient monitoring; pressure alone cannot establish adequate tissue oxygenation.

Subsections in the source

  • 6.1 Safe perfusion equipment. Measure arterial flow delivered to the patient, pressure, oxygenation, blood gases, haematocrit and temperature.
  • 6.2 Brain monitoring. Regional cerebral saturation (NIRS), processed EEG/depth of anaesthesia and, in selected settings, transcranial Doppler.
  • 6.3 Haemodynamic monitoring. Pulmonary artery catheter, pulse-contour analysis and transoesophageal echocardiography are discussed for selected roles.

Three recommendations worth retaining

  1. Patient-directed arterial flow measurement: ultrasonic arterial-line flow measurement is recommended (Class I, level C; Table 16).
  2. Continuous SvO₂ and haematocrit: recommended during CPB (Class I, level B; Table 16); blood gas analysis should be periodic or continuous (Class I, level C).
  3. Detect cerebral hypoperfusion: a NIRS-guided algorithm should be considered (Class IIa, level B; Table 17). This does not establish proven improvement in all neurological outcomes.

Module 2 · Pump flow & DO₂ (sections 9.5 and 9.7)

In one sentence: An acceptable MAP does not guarantee adequate oxygen delivery; assess both flow and arterial oxygen content, then follow metabolic indicators.

What the sections address

  • 9.5 Pump flow management. Flow is initially estimated from body surface area and temperature, then assessed with oxygenation/metabolic signals. The text describes a customary 2.2–2.8 L/min/m² range under moderate hypothermia to normothermia—not a universal fixed target.
  • 9.7 Goal-directed perfusion. Avoid both a low nadir of DO₂ and prolonged exposure to low DO₂ during CPB.

Three recommendations worth retaining

  1. Minimum indexed DO₂ ≥280 mL/min/m² is recommended to reduce stage 1 acute kidney injury (Class I, level A; Table 41).
  2. During normothermic CPB, considering a lower DO₂ threshold of 280–300 mL/min/m² as part of goal-directed perfusion is advised (Class IIa, level B; Table 43).
  3. Goal-directed perfusion to reduce early AKI is recommended (Class I, level A; Table 43). Markers include SvO₂, extraction ratio, cerebral oxygenation and lactate trends; no single validated threshold applies to every marker (Table 41).

Practical retrieval question: If MAP is satisfactory but indexed DO₂ is low, how would flow, Hb and SaO₂ influence the assessment? Which measurement is reliable and available during the actual case?

Source: same co-published guideline, text tables 41 and 43. The target is not a guarantee of adequate organ perfusion in every patient.
